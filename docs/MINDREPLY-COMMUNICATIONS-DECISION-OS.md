# MindReply Communications & Decision-Layer OS

**Version:** 1.0 — 2026-09-27  
**Purpose:** Establish one clear public/product direction for MindReply.

## Position

MindReply is an owner-controlled decision and execution layer for businesses that need clearer choices, safer changes, and measurable movement.

Public promise:

> Think clearly. Decide fast. Move forward.

The product does not sell more automation as the primary outcome. It sells better decisions, controlled execution, and visible proof.

## Public message hierarchy

**Clarity → Decision → Action → Proof**

Supporting ideas:
- Turn complex operational problems into a bounded decision.
- Convert approved decisions into an implementation sequence.
- Keep consequential changes reviewable and reversible.
- Show evidence rather than simulated progress.
- Keep the human owner accountable for consequential release decisions.

Avoid generic AI-first positioning, unsupported autonomy claims, fake telemetry or metrics, public exposure of internal orchestration names, and set-and-forget language for consequential operations.

## Product architecture

### Signal
Capture the business problem, context, constraints, and desired outcome.

### Decision Layer
Separate facts, assumptions, constraints, options, dependencies, risks, and expected outcomes. Produce a bounded decision record.

### Execution Layer
Compile the approved decision into ordered actions, owners, dependencies, acceptance criteria, rollback path, and evidence requirements.

### Proof Layer
Record what changed, when, who approved it, what evidence exists, whether the expected result occurred, and what remains blocked.

## Customer experience

1. Frame — define the real problem.
2. Clarify — establish evidence and constraints.
3. Decide — compare realistic paths.
4. Approve — owner confirms consequential action.
5. Execute — perform only the approved scope.
6. Verify — test the resulting state.
7. Record — preserve evidence and decision history.

No consequential production action should be implied merely because a recommendation was generated.

## Trust contract

MindReply should make three things obvious:

**What is known.** Direct evidence and source.

**What is proposed.** A decision or action awaiting approval.

**What is blocked.** A required dependency or evidence path is unavailable.

Internal operating status must resolve to VERIFIED, FAILED, or BLOCKED. UNVERIFIED is not an accepted operating status.

## Commercial structure

The public product should lead with outcomes rather than internal architecture.

Potential offer ladder:
- Decision Review — one bounded problem, evidence review, options, decision record.
- Execution Pack — approved decision converted into implementation sequence, controls, and verification.
- Operator Retainer — recurring review, decision support, execution governance, and evidence continuity.

Pricing and availability must only be published when directly supported by the current commercial system.

## Decision-language rules

Use:
- recommended path
- approved
- ready for review
- evidence required
- release
- rollback
- owner decision

Avoid:
- the system decided, for consequential decisions;
- fully autonomous;
- guaranteed;
- live without current runtime evidence;
- metrics without a traceable source.

## Definition of a strong MindReply interaction

A strong interaction leaves the owner with:

**One problem clearly framed.  
One decision clearly stated.  
One approved next move.  
One evidence path.  
One recovery path.**

That is the communications and decision-layer standard.
